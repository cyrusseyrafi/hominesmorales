# *Homines morales*: A Game-Theoretic Analysis of the Evolutionary Fitness of Moral Philosophies

## Abstract
 
Neoclassical economic theory presupposes that individual behavior is unilaterally driven by the maximization of material self-interest, but advances in behavioral economics reveal that real-world behavior stems from a far wider range of motivations. This paper asks whether individuals guided by ethical theories from a range of moral philosophical traditions can outcompete material self-interest maximization in evolutionary settings.
 
Building upon Alger and Weibull (2013), I formalize the moral philosophies of John Stuart Mill, Immanuel Kant, and Adam Smith as transformations of material payoff functions, derive the Nash equilibria each type plays against the others and against the *homo economicus* in the Prisoner's Dilemma and Hawk-Dove Game, and study which types survive under replicator dynamics.
 
In the Hawk-Dove Game, adherents to Mill's philosophy take over every population they enter. In the Prisoner's Dilemma, outcomes depend on payoffs, but in most cases adherents to Smith's philosophy outperform the *homo economicus*. Future research may extend these results to further game-theoretic strategies and scenarios.
